\section{Circut to light a LED on}
\label{sec:org71e55b1}

A red LED can turn with 1.2v and 5 to 20mA of current, but the Arduino
works with a current of 5v and very high current. To prevent the
led from recieving damage we would add a resistant to it.

\subsection{Formula for this:}
\label{sec:orgb68cbfb}

\[
R = \frac{V_s - V_1}{I}
\]

The elements on this formula are
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(V_s\) is the current is the suply voltage (5v)
\item \(V_1\) is the LED forward voltage drop (1.7v)
\item \(I\)   is the current required by the led (10mA) in Amp so, (0.001A)
\end{enumerate}

Replacing the values on the formula we would find that

\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{5\,\text{V} - 1.7\,\text{V}}{0.001\,\text{A}}\\
R &= \frac{3.3\,\text{V}}{0.001\,\text{A}}\\
R &= 330\,\Omega
\end{aligned}

The general formula to the this is:

\begin{center}
V = I * R
\end{center}
V for voltage
I for current
R for resistance
